# Catch a selected vehicle: backend contract

The web process selects a vehicle from its own `combineVehicleSnapshots` collector result. It sends that vehicle as `{ "snapshot": selectedVehicle }` to the private routing origin's `POST /api/internal/vehicles/upcoming`. This endpoint is not part of the public routing proxy allowlist. Browser-provided snapshots must never be forwarded as trusted collector data. Rider coordinates belong to the separate journey-planning request, not this endpoint.

The adapter in `backend/catch-vehicle.mjs` consumes the collector's actual plain `agencyId`, raw `id` and `tripId`, qualified `vehicleKey`, ISO `timestamp`, optional publisher `startDate`, and optional `nextStopId`. It bounds identifiers to 200 UTF-8 bytes, rejects controls, conflicting agency prefixes and stale observations, and emits no vehicle coordinates. Freshness allows at most 120 seconds of age and 30 seconds of future clock skew. A collector-marked stale vehicle is rejected independently.

Published service dates must be real `YYYYMMDD` dates on the current or previous Toronto calendar day. This preserves service continuing after midnight. When the publisher omits `startDate`, only the Toronto calendar date of the observation is queried and `serviceDateSource` is explicitly `observation-calendar`; it is not evidence of the publisher's service day. A failed exact lookup stays unavailable. Feed version selection uses the dated `data/feeds.json` catalog, including the TTC static-feed transition. This adapter does not join TTC's incompatible realtime trip identifiers or enable TTC routing updates.

The backend queries OTP for that exact trip and date. A missing or mismatched trip returns `state: unavailable` and no candidates. When the publisher supplies a next-stop identity, it anchors the stop sequence to that exact stop; a missing anchor yields no candidates. Otherwise only future published times are considered. The search inspects at most 40 downstream stops and returns at most three with numeric, in-range coordinates and an exact feed-qualified identity. Canceled and skipped stops are excluded. A candidate's `basis` is `realtime` when its selected time is published as realtime, otherwise `scheduled`. `arriveByAt` is that time minus a 120-second safety margin. No vehicle speed extrapolation, proximity match, guessed stop or invented journey is used.

The web process must then plan the rider's route to each candidate with its `arriveByAt`, select a feasible complete itinerary, and keep all downstream stops and onward destinations intact. Upcoming-stop results alone do not prove the rider can catch the vehicle. Responses use `Cache-Control: no-store`.

## Verification

`tests/catch-vehicle-backend.test.mjs` encodes a GTFS-Realtime fixture, passes it through the real `parseVehicleFeed` and `combineVehicleSnapshots` functions, and exercises valid identities, prefix conflicts, bounds, freshness, Toronto midnight/DST, stop anchoring and prediction basis. `tests/live-coverage-http.test.mjs` starts the flattened backend using the actual Dockerfile COPY layout, submits a real collector-shaped snapshot over HTTP, verifies the three-stop response and tests invalid bodies without contacting OTP. Its OTP responses are local test fixtures, not a claim that a deployed graph has been verified.
