# Catch a selected vehicle: backend contract

The web process selects a vehicle from its own `combineVehicleSnapshots` collector result. It sends that vehicle as `{ "snapshot": selectedVehicle }` to the private routing origin's `POST /api/internal/vehicles/upcoming`. This endpoint is not part of the public routing proxy allowlist. Browser-provided snapshots must never be forwarded as trusted collector data. Rider coordinates belong to the separate journey-planning request, not this endpoint.

The adapter in `backend/catch-vehicle.mjs` consumes the collector's actual plain `agencyId`, raw vehicle, route and optional trip identifiers, qualified `vehicleKey`, ISO `timestamp`, optional publisher `startDate`, and optional `nextStopId`. Position alignment additionally requires the collector's numeric latitude, longitude and bearing. It bounds identifiers to 200 UTF-8 bytes, rejects controls, conflicting agency prefixes and stale observations, and strips vehicle coordinates from its response. Freshness allows at most 120 seconds of age and 30 seconds of future clock skew. A collector-marked stale vehicle is rejected independently.

Published service dates must be real `YYYYMMDD` dates on the current or previous Toronto calendar day. This preserves service continuing after midnight. When the publisher omits `startDate`, the exact lookup uses the Toronto calendar date of the observation and `serviceDateSource` is explicitly `observation-calendar`; it is not evidence of the publisher's service day. Feed version selection uses the dated `data/feeds.json` catalog, including the TTC static-feed transition. This adapter does not rewrite TTC's incompatible realtime trip identifiers or enable TTC routing updates.

The backend first queries OTP for the exact trip and date when a trip ID exists. A successful identity join retains `method: trip-id` and `confirmedTrip: true`. The publisher's next-stop identity anchors that sequence when present. Canceled and skipped stops are excluded. Exact-trip candidates retain `basis: realtime` or `scheduled`, and their `arriveByAt` is the selected time minus 120 seconds.

When the exact trip does not join, `patternAlignedTimesWithOtp` can use the trusted collector position against actual ordered source-pattern segments. Dated route anchors are cached for ten minutes, with at most 64 cache entries. A segment must be within 150 metres and its direction must agree with the reported bearing within 60 degrees. Multiple matching patterns, unresolved overlapping segments, missing bearing and off-pattern observations never silently select an arbitrary result.

The alignment path makes at most two OTP calls: one bounded departure lookup at the downstream anchor, followed by the nearest route-matching timetable trip's stop times. Together with the optional failed exact lookup this is at most three calls under one eight-second budget. Equally near trips remain ambiguous. The returned trip's consecutive source stops and downstream pattern must agree exactly. The chosen timetable's own `serviceDay` determines the queried date, including after-midnight service. Scheduled travel time between the segment endpoints is interpolated at the reported position to calculate `offsetSeconds`; downstream scheduled times receive that same offset. This uses a timetable, never vehicle-speed extrapolation or an invented schedule.

Every aligned candidate has `basis: aligned-timetable` and `alignedArrival`, with `alignment { patternId, directionId, anchorStopId, offsetSeconds, distanceMetres }`. Both response and reference trip are explicitly unconfirmed. Original realtime predictions are not relabelled as these estimates. The required disclosure is: **Arrival times for this vehicle are timetable estimates aligned to the bus's reported position, not publisher predictions.** The reference timetable trip is not a confirmed vehicle assignment. Missing, inconsistent, cancelled or backwards times are not fabricated into usable estimates.

The web composite accepts `{ vehicleKey, origin: { lat, lon, name? }, preferences?: { wheelchair?, maxWalkDistance? } }`. It selects the vehicle from its own fresh collector data and never accepts a browser-supplied snapshot. It considers at most three candidate stops within 15 km whose safety deadline leaves more than 60 seconds. Each normal planning request uses `arriveBy: true`, `dateTime: arriveByAt`, `preference: fastest`, `allowDirectWalking: true`, and the current accessibility preferences. The default walking limit is 2,500 metres. At most two planning calls run concurrently under the existing shared 25-second deadline and cancellation signal.

Feasible walking and transit connections return an explicit `journey`. A `walk` alias exists only when every leg is WALK. Chronology, exact destination identity when present, endpoint coordinates, walking limits and the start-time tolerance of 30 seconds are verified. Connections using the selected vehicle, its exact published trip or its current route are excluded conservatively. Repeated endpoints and inconsistent transit identities cannot become a catchable result. Each option retains its basis, arrival deadline, slack, margin and leave-in timing; aligned options retain the disclosure and `confirmedTrip: false`. Upcoming-stop results alone never prove the rider can catch the vehicle. Responses use `Cache-Control: no-store`.

## Verification

`tests/catch-vehicle-backend.test.mjs` encodes a GTFS-Realtime fixture, passes it through the real `parseVehicleFeed` and `combineVehicleSnapshots` functions, and exercises valid identities, prefix conflicts, bounds, freshness, Toronto midnight/DST, stop anchoring and prediction basis. `tests/live-coverage-http.test.mjs` starts the flattened backend using the actual Dockerfile COPY layout, submits a real collector-shaped snapshot over HTTP, verifies the three-stop response and tests invalid bodies without contacting OTP. Its OTP responses are local test fixtures, not a claim that a deployed graph has been verified.

`tests/pattern-aligned-times.test.mjs` exercises source-index normalization, radius and bearing boundaries, ambiguity, offset arithmetic, service-date crossover, cancellation and the three-call ceiling through real local HTTP. `tests/intercept.test.mjs` verifies arrive-by requests, accessibility forwarding, genuine normalized planner responses, mixed-mode feasibility, self-use rejection and mandatory estimate labelling. Physical-device and live deployed-pattern verification remain separate evidence.
